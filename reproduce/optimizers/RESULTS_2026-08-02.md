# Hot-start optimizer study — results, 2026-08-02

Archive: `reproduce/outputs/opt-hotcold-2026-08-02/`
Protocol: Concrete, 2nd-order consequents, 144 antecedent parameters, 10 seeds,
2,000 objective evaluations per arm per seed, trust-region radius 1.0 (the full
box), single-threaded, budget enforced by a wrapper that raises.

The study asks two questions. How much can an optimizer improve the fuzzy model
in a fixed number of iterations, and how much of that depends on starting from
the Gaussian construction rather than from nowhere.

---

## The short version

1. **The construction's parameter *placement* is worth nothing measurable here.**
   A uniform random draw from the same box scores as well as the fitted model
   before any optimization: paired **+0.010 ± 0.033 R², random winning 6/10
   seeds**.
2. **What the construction is worth, in iterations, is about three.** A
   population method starting from a random point matches the construction's
   objective in 2–3 evaluations. A local method needs ~250.
3. **No optimizer is separable from any other.** Every arm-vs-arm paired
   difference in held-out R² is smaller than its own spread.
4. **The objective everything is optimized against does not track
   generalization.** Three independent signs of it, below.

Point 1 has a load-bearing qualification, and it is the first thing to read.

---

## What "cold" does and does not randomize

A cold run draws its antecedents uniformly from `build_param_bounds(model,
X_train)`. Those bounds put each centre inside its feature's observed range and
each width inside a sensible fraction of that range — and, critically, the
*shape* of the parameter vector comes from the fitted model: which features were
retained, how many mixture components sit on each, for which output bucket.

So a cold start inherits everything the construction discovered about
**structure** and randomizes only **placement and width**. This experiment
therefore decomposes the construction's contribution and measures one half of
it:

| what the construction supplies | measured here? | worth |
|---|---|---|
| structure — which features, how many components | **no** | untested |
| placement and width of each component | yes | ≈ 0.00 R² |

The untested half is what `run_structure_study.py` exists for. Until that lands,
nothing here licenses the sentence "the Gaussian construction is unnecessary".

## 1. Before any optimization

The `none` arm at zero evaluations — just scoring the starting point.

| starting point | test R² | CV MSE |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gaussian construction | 0.8408 ± 0.0338 | 0.00639 |
| random draw, same box | 0.8511 ± 0.0229 | 0.00619 |

Paired, random − construction: **+0.0103 ± 0.0325 R²**, random better on 6/10
seeds; **−0.00020 ± 0.00056 CV MSE**, random better on 5/10. Indistinguishable
in both directions, which is the point: the construction is not *ahead*.

The likely mechanism is that the consequents are re-solved in closed form for
whatever antecedents are present. Given a partition of the input space that is
merely sensible — in range, sensibly scaled — the ridge-TSK solve appears to
absorb the difference. That is a claim about this dataset at this consequent
order and should be read as one.

## 2. The price of not having the construction

Evaluations before a cold run's objective matched what the construction supplies
for free (mean ± s.d. over 10 seeds):

| arm | evaluations |
|---|---:|
| scipy-de | 2 ± 2 |
| opt-ga | 3 ± 8 |
| opt-pso | 3 ± 8 |
| opt-aco | 3 ± 8 |
| scipy-powell | 239 ± 371 |
| scipy-lbfgsb | 272 ± 303 |
| opt-gd | 272 ± 303 |

The split is between population and local methods, and it is large. A population
method draws a spread of candidates immediately and one of them is already at
the construction's level; a local method has to walk there, and with 144
parameters a single finite-difference gradient costs ~144 evaluations, so ~250
is roughly two gradient steps.

Read as "what is the construction worth", the answer at this budget is **two to
three evaluations if you are going to run a population method afterwards**.

## 3. After the full budget

At 2,000 evaluations, held-out R², and the paired gain over the construction:

| arm | hot R² | cold R² | hot Δ vs heuristic | cold Δ vs heuristic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| scipy-lbfgsb | 0.854 ± 0.044 | 0.869 ± 0.020 | +0.013 ± 0.029 | +0.028 ± 0.038 |
| scipy-powell | 0.861 ± 0.028 | 0.866 ± 0.027 | +0.020 ± 0.041 | +0.025 ± 0.040 |
| scipy-de | 0.863 ± 0.022 | 0.863 ± 0.022 | +0.022 ± 0.036 | +0.022 ± 0.036 |
| opt-ga | 0.875 ± 0.021 | 0.869 ± 0.025 | +0.034 ± 0.023 | +0.028 ± 0.032 |
| opt-pso | 0.867 ± 0.020 | 0.871 ± 0.019 | +0.026 ± 0.022 | +0.030 ± 0.031 |
| opt-aco | 0.872 ± 0.015 | 0.872 ± 0.015 | +0.030 ± 0.032 | +0.031 ± 0.031 |
| opt-gd | 0.854 ± 0.044 | 0.867 ± 0.024 | +0.013 ± 0.029 | +0.026 ± 0.038 |

Two things to notice. The cold column is not worse — for the two local methods
it is *better* (L-BFGS-B 0.869 cold against 0.854 hot). And every arm improves
on the construction by +0.013 to +0.034, on 6–10 seeds out of 10.

**No ordering survives pairing.** GA against L-BFGS-B, hot, is +0.020 ± 0.040
with 7/10 seeds won; every other pair is smaller relative to its spread. The
ranking in the table above is not a result and must not be quoted as one.

## 4. The objective is not tracking generalization

Three independent sightings in this one study:

- **Conversion rates differ ~3.6×.** L-BFGS-B removes 31% of the CV objective
  and gains +0.004 R² per 10% removed; PSO removes 17% and gains +0.015 per 10%.
  The arms that drive the objective hardest bank the least of it.
- **Hot L-BFGS-B beats cold L-BFGS-B on the objective and loses on R²** (0.00445
  vs 0.00515 CV; 0.854 vs 0.869 R²).
- **A cold start needs 272 evaluations to match the construction's objective but
  is already level with it on test R² at zero.**

This is §6.3.5's overfitting-the-cross-validation-estimate mechanism, and here it
points at the *local* methods rather than the population ones.

---

## What this does and does not say about §6.3.5

§6.3.5 says a plain local optimizer usually beats the population methods, and
that a global search has very little left to find.

- **"L-BFGS-B beats the population methods": not supported here.** Also not
  reversed — the paired differences are noise. The honest status is that the
  ordering is unresolved.
- **"Very little left to find": in tension.** +0.02 to +0.03 R² on a 0.841
  baseline is small but consistent, found by every arm on most seeds.
- **Neither point is refuted, because this is a different regime.** §6.3.5's DE
  arm runs `maxiter=40, popsize=8` over 144 parameters — roughly 46,000
  evaluations against this study's 2,000. Twenty-three times the budget. The
  chapter's text should not be edited on this evidence; the budget sweep should
  be run first.

## Next, in order

1. **The structure study** (`run_structure_study.py`, built, not yet run). It
   measures the half of the construction's contribution this study could not.
2. **Widen the box.** If placement is worth nothing inside
   `build_param_bounds`, the natural question is where it starts to matter.
   Loosening the width band and re-running cold isolates scale from placement.
3. **Match §6.3.5's budget** (~46,000 evaluations) before touching Chapter 6's
   text.
4. **A second dataset.** Everything here is Concrete at one consequent order.

# Addendum — identification: the old way against the new one

Archive: `reproduce/outputs/opt-identification-2026-08-02/`
Concrete, 3 seeds, rule counts {2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12}, median of 3 repeats,
**single-threaded** (OMP/BLAS pinned before numpy imports), k-means `n_init=10`.

Both routes are asked for the same number of rules at every point. The rule
count is an input to the classical route and normally an output of the
construction, so comparing a 3-rule construction against a 9-rule classical
model would compare capacity rather than identification.

## What was found

**On cost the result is not close, and it is not in the construction's favour.**

| rules | construction | classical k-means | classical FCM |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 471 ± 4 ms | 12 ± 1 ms | 7 ± 1 ms |
| 8 | 1172 ± 27 ms | 22 ± 3 ms | 39 ± 2 ms |
| 12 | 1743 ± 32 ms | 26 ± 2 ms | 69 ± 4 ms |

Between **25× and 84× faster**, at every rule count, with spreads of a few
milliseconds. The construction's time goes on its $O(M^2)$ feature screening and
on fitting an automatically-chosen number of mixture components per (feature,
bucket); the classical route runs one clustering and projects it.

**On accuracy the classical route is not worse, and probably slightly better —
but three seeds cannot settle it.** Paired at matched rule count, pooled over
all six counts and three seeds (n = 18):

| | Δ R² vs construction | wins |
|---|---:|---:|
| classical k-means | +0.022 ± 0.075 | 13/18 |
| classical FCM | +0.026 ± 0.057 | 13/18 |

Both means are smaller than their own spreads. Individual rule counts at c = 4
and c = 6 fall outside spread in the classical route's favour, but at three
seeds that is weak evidence and should not be quoted as an ordering.

**On parameters the gap is arithmetic rather than statistical.** At matched rule
count the construction carries about three times the free antecedent parameters
— 142 against 48 at c = 3, 467 against 192 at c = 12 — because it fits several
mixture components per (feature, bucket) where the classical route places
exactly one Gaussian per (feature, rule). So panel (a)'s rough parity is
achieved by the classical route with a third of the capacity.

## What this does not establish

- **One dataset, and a small one.** Concrete is 824 × 8 after the split.
  k-means on that costs nothing; the construction's cost is dominated by fitting
  candidate mixtures rather than by row count. The two scale differently and the
  ordering could move on a large problem — which makes PhiUSIIL (235K × 54) the
  test that could overturn this, not merely a bigger example.
- **Three seeds.** Enough for the timing, which has millisecond spreads. Not
  enough for the accuracy comparison.
- **The construction picks its own rule count; the classical route is told.**
  Sweeping c is what makes the comparison fair, but it also hands the classical
  route a choice the construction makes for itself, and that is worth something
  the accuracy column does not show.
- **Nothing here is about the optimizer.** This is identification only. What an
  optimizer does afterwards, from either starting point, is the study above.

## Where that leaves the thesis claim

The expectation going in was that the construction would beat k-means and FCM
handily on time and be solid on initial accuracy. On this dataset the second
half holds only in the restricted placement comparison (`clusterinit`, where the
construction is ahead by +0.008 against k-means and +0.022 against FCM at ten
seeds), and the first half is contradicted outright by the identification sweep.

The honest reading is that the construction's advantage is not speed of
identification, and on Concrete it is not initial accuracy either. What it does
supply, which none of the comparisons above credit it for, is a rule count and a
feature set derived from the data rather than chosen. Whether that is worth its
cost is a question this study has not yet asked.
