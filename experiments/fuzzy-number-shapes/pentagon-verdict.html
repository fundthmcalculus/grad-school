Pentagon footprint verdict

Does a pentagonal footprint of uncertainty give better intervals than a simple residual interval?

No, not in this setup.

The pentagon footprint loses to a kNN residual interval on the same point predictor, on 9 of 10 seeds. Its only edge is a slightly better point prediction, and that does not survive a longer optimiser run.

Robustness: longer optimiser

The calibrated pentagon stopped at its 40-iteration cap in the main run. Re-running it with a cap of 200 on seeds 0–2 shows the result is fragile.

Recommendation

  1. Do not use the pentagon footprint for intervals in problems like this one.
  2. If an interval is needed, use a type-1 point and a kNN residual quantile. It is simpler, faster and better calibrated here.
  3. Revisit only with a held-out ridge and a fixed optimiser budget, tuned on separate seeds, with the point and interval effects measured separately.

Caveats

  • One target function, K = 5, 500 training points. Results may differ elsewhere.
  • The consequent ridge (1e-2) was chosen on seeds 0–1, which are also in the evaluation.
  • Calibration hit its iteration cap in the main run, so the pentagon numbers are a lower bound on what calibration can achieve.
  • The baselines use in-sample residuals. A held-out calibration could tighten them slightly, but not by the 0.13 gap observed.
  • The kNN baseline uses x. Where noise is not a function of x it reduces to the global interval, and the ranking could change.
