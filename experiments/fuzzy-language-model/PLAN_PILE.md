## Findings from the scaffold
1. **Pythia's 50,277-token vocabulary cannot fit under 100K parameters.** The fuzzy tail table alone puts the model at 437,615 parameters at 50,277 tokens with tail width 8 (head 640, d32, two rules), 238,939 at width 4 and 139,601 at width 2, against a cap of 99,999 (6,144 tokens at width 8: 84,551). The Pile run therefore uses a BPE trained on the Pile sample (6,144 or 8,192 tokens, `--tokenizer bpe:<V>`). Bits per character does not depend on the tokenizer, so the comparison with Pythia is unaffected.
2. **Text is folded to 98 symbols** (tab to space, carriage return dropped, everything else outside printable ASCII to a question mark), as for the other corpora, so the denominator is exact. Pythia has to be scored on the same folded validation text for a like-for-like number; `flm/score_pythia.py` takes the raw corpora now, and a `--text` option for the folded file is the next small change (not done).
3. **A token can span more than 255 characters** (runs of dashes in the Pile), so token lengths are stored as 16-bit. The first version stored them as 8-bit and the length check (token lengths against the characters of the text) caught a 2-character mismatch in 5.7M. The check stays.
4. **Quotas are soft**: a shard stops at the first document past its character quota, and one Pile document can be megabytes (shard 0 gave 3.7M characters against a 2.0M quota in the smoke run). Fine for a sample; cap the document length if equal shard weights matter.
5. The mirror has a `text` column only (no `meta`), so the per-document Pile component is not available from this source.

## Training configuration for the Pile (a procedure for the other machine; not run here)
Registered before anything is run on this corpus.
1. **Corpus.** `python -m flm.prep_pile --name pile6144 --shards 40 --train-chars 330e6 --valid-chars 4e6 --tokenizer bpe:6144` (and a second corpus name with `bpe:8192`). Check the manifest: characters per token, any quota shortfall warning, and that the token-length check passed.
2. **Candidates** come from the random search (`PLAN_ROUND3.md` stage 4): its best three allocations (vocabulary, head, tail, d, mixer rules, rule split), fitted again for the Pile vocabulary with `flm.random_shape.fit_rules`, plus F7 itself as the reference (`P_bpe6144` and `P_bpe8192` in `report/pile_arms.json`).
3. **Screen**: each candidate at 30M characters, three seeds, warm-up 600, lr 6e-2, batch 16, floor 0, wd 0.01 (the recipe that survived 300M; the batch-8 lever did not). A candidate replaces F7 only if it beats F7 by 0.005 on two of three seeds.
4. **Confirm** the winner at 300M (two seeds, warm-up 1000), then score Pythia-14M on the same folded validation text and report bits per character against parameters. With a bigger GPU, re-measure throughput first; a larger batch is its own arm with its own learning-rate screen (the stage-2 lesson).

Predictions for the 30M screen on the Pile sample: F7's bits per character is above its TinyStories value of 1.05 by at least 1.5 (90%: the Pile is far harder than stories); the ranking of the allocations on the Pile differs from the ranking on TinyStories for at least one of the top three (65%); head size matters less on the Pile than on TinyStories (50%).

## First Pile training run: the 14M model on the streamed sample (owner's go, 2026-10-23; registered before it runs)
Corpus `pile6144`: 40 evenly spaced shards of `EleutherAI/the_pile_deduplicated` (revision `fcbfcfde…`), 330M folded characters for training and 4M for validation (documents split by hash), a BPE of 6,144 tokens trained on the first 30M folded characters (`python -m flm.prep_pile --name pile6144 --shards 40 --train-chars 330e6 --valid-chars 4e6 --tokenizer bpe:6144 --fit-chars 30e6`; mixed licences, internal comparison only). Model: `M14_v6144_h1024_d384_l4` (14,067,715 parameters, the same shape as the TinyStories run), 300M characters, warm-up 1000, **lr 4e-2** (the middle of the plateau found on TinyStories at 30M; not re-screened on this corpus), batch 16, context 512, floor 0, fp32, one seed, checkpoint and resume every 1,000 steps (`--ckpt-dir outputs/v84-pile14m/ckpt`), evaluation every 1,000 steps, W&B off (no login). The 1,024 head tokens are the most frequent tokens of the Pile sample.
Then (CPU, while it trains) Pythia-14M is scored on the same folded validation text (`python -m flm.score_pythia --folded-dir data/pile6144/text`).
Predictions: characters per token of the Pile BPE6144 on the validation text 2.8-3.3 (75%); about 12,000 training steps and 45-75 minutes (70%); no divergence at lr 4e-2 over the whole run (80%); validation bits per character after 300M characters between 1.5 and 2.1 (65%); below 1.8 (40%); **Pythia-14M, scored zero-shot-in-distribution on the same text, beats our model** (92%), by at least 0.15 bits per character (65%) (it saw about 1,000 times more text); our TinyStories-tuned lr 4e-2 is within 0.05 of the best rate on this corpus (50%, untested here); the run resumes from a checkpoint to the same loss if killed and restarted (90%, not exercised unless it is interrupted).

## First Pile run of the 14M model: result (recorded 2026-10-23; `outputs/v84-pile14m`)
**Validation 1.9695 bits per character** on the folded Pile validation text (1.9694 re-scored in fp32), after 300M characters (300,000,805; 11,417 steps), one seed. **Pythia-14M scores 1.3323 on the same text** (`report/pythia_pile_folded.json`), so it leads by 0.637 bits per character; it was trained on about 1,000 times more text, so this says how far a 300M-character budget is from Pythia's, not that the design is worse per token seen. Curve (validation): 2.4351, 2.2999, 2.2403, 2.1997, 2.1633, 2.1344, 2.0861, 2.0530, 2.0134, 1.9817, 1.9700, 1.9695 at steps 1,000 to 11,000 and the end.
Time: 2,529 s of training in all (990 s in fp32 to step 4,000, then 1,539 s in bf16); the bf16 part ran 208 s per 1,000 steps against 241 s in fp32 (1.16x, 39,400 against 34,000 tokens per second). Wall clock from start to finish about 66 minutes, including the stalled first attempt and two restarts.
What went wrong and was fixed: the first attempt (fp32) slowed to a crawl after its step-1,000 evaluation: the process held 11.2 GB of the 12.3 GB card plus about 1 GB in shared memory (the evaluation at batch 64 and the cached blocks it left). It was stopped and resumed from the step-1,000 checkpoint with `--eval-batch 16` and the cache released after each evaluation and checkpoint (peak 5.3 GB allocated, 6.7-7.1 GB on the card, no shared memory); the owner then asked to switch to bf16, which was done from the step-4,000 checkpoint with `--resume-ignore precision` (printed, not silent). The loss curve shows no step at either switch. Checkpoint and resume is therefore exercised twice, but its bit-exact equivalence to an uninterrupted run was only tested on the small CPU model, not on this run.
Prediction scoring: characters per token of the Pile BPE6144 on the validation text 2.8-3.3 (75%): **right** (3.14 training, 3.19 validation); about 12,000 steps and 45-75 minutes (70%): **steps right (11,417), time narrowly wrong** (42 minutes of training; 66 minutes of wall clock with the stall); no divergence at lr 4e-2 (80%): **right**; validation between 1.5 and 2.1 (65%): **right** (1.9695); below 1.8 (40%): **wrong**; Pythia-14M beats our model (92%): **right**, by at least 0.15 (65%): **right** (0.637); lr 4e-2 within 0.05 of the best on this corpus (50%): **untested**; resume to the same loss if killed (90%): **worked, equivalence not measured on this run**.
