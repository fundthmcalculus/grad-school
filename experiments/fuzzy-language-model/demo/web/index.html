Most used rules

Rules are ranked by how often each is the strongest at a position, over 48 validation windows. The pieces listed are the ones the rule raises most. Click a rule to inspect it.

Each panel is one of the 16 input dimensions of this rule. The curve is the rule's membership function on that dimension (its centre and width); the rule's firing is the product of these 16 curves. Gray bars show where the data falls on that dimension; teal bars show where it falls when this rule fires strongly. Panels are ordered from most to least selective (data spread divided by membership width): a high value means the rule only accepts a narrow part of the data on that dimension.

What to expect

These are tiny models (about 100 thousand parameters; the third entry has 1.3 million for comparison) trained only on simple children's stories (TinyStories). They write fluent, short, childlike sentences, then often drift, repeat or lose the plot after a few sentences: in blind ratings the 96K-class models scored about 5 to 8 out of 15 for coherence and the 1.3 million model about 10. They know nothing outside that story world, and nothing here is reviewed for accuracy. The seeded sampler is JavaScript's, so a seed does not reproduce the Python runs in the write-up.

Source, training scripts and results: experiments/fuzzy-language-model in the grad-school repository (WRITEUP.md). Everything on this page runs on your device; nothing you type is sent anywhere.
