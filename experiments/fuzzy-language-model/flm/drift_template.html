Dictionary drift

The constructed 64-dimensional character dictionary, and where each symbol ends up after the model trains it for 300 million characters (invertible model, 100K parameters, seed 0). If training left the geometry alone, every dot in the first charts would sit on the diagonal.

Do distances between characters survive training?

Each dot is a pair of characters: its distance in the constructed dictionary (across) and after training (up), each divided by its own average. Left: trained from the constructed start. Right: control, trained from a random start, which the constructed dictionary has no reason to resemble.

Which characters moved?

Each tile is one character. The number is how well its distances to all other characters are preserved (1.00 means unchanged); a darker tile is better kept. Under it, how many of its five nearest neighbours are the same before and after.

Inside one character

All 64 coordinates of the chosen character, as the model started with them (hollow bars) and after training (filled bars), in the same frame. Click any tile above, or a letter here. The blocks are the layout: class, letter identity, digit value, vowel, punctuation group, symbol code, special, whitespace, kind.

The map, and the move

The constructed dictionary flattened to its two main directions. Each arrow goes from a character's constructed position to where training put it (rotated and scaled into the same frame). The flattening hides the other 62 directions, so the arrows understate the movement.
