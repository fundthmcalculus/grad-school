What the fuzzy model writes

A 93K-parameter fuzzy model with a 1,024-token vocabulary now scores 1.05 bits per character after 100M characters, and it fits under the 100K cap. The stories look like stories and still are not coherent. Read the samples, then see what moved and what did not.

Verdict. The 93K subword model (1.055 BPC at 100M characters; the 30M checkpoint is shown) and the 150K one writes dialogue and plot-turn sentences the character models do not, and beats the 280K neural teacher on loss. It still loses track of who is who ("Lily wanted to play with Lily"), and it invents more words (1.5% against under 1%). Loss improved by 0.11 and coherence by far less. Judging that needs a grader, not a loss.

What is wrong with the text

  • Spelling is mostly solved. At temperature 0.7 with top-k 20, under 1% of words are non-words for the character models and 1.5% for the subword model, whose pieces sometimes recombine into words that do not exist ("groceri", "bretty").
  • Reference errors remain. "Lily and her mom were both very happy" follows "She wanted to play with Lily" in a story about Lily; the character models bring in a new character from nowhere or swap names mid-story. Subwords fixed word shapes and sentence rhythm, not who is who.
  • Higher temperature trades coherence for variety. Temperature 1.0 triples the non-word rate and makes the grammar worse, so use the lower setting for any review.

Did post-training or size help?

Validation bits per character, first 1M characters of TinyStories V2 validation. 2 seeds for the 97K model. The only thing that helped is more training and more parameters.

  • Continuing training: +10M characters at a low learning rate gives −0.027 BPC. That is exactly what the pretraining curve predicts, so it is not a separate technique.
  • Weight averaging (EMA): −0.0005. No effect.
  • Distillation from the 280K teacher: no effect (+0.001). The teacher is better by 0.15 BPC but its soft labels carry little beyond the text itself at this scale.
  • Bigger models: 162K reaches 1.217 against 1.275 at 97K. Gains taper: the last +23K parameters bought −0.018.

Getting to coherent text

Coherence is about holding a story together across a few hundred characters: who is in it, what they own, what happens next. A character-level model spends most of its capacity on spelling and word boundaries. Here is what I would try, in order of expected value per hour of GPU.

  1. Subword tokens: done, and it worked on loss. A 512-token vocabulary gave 1.103 BPC at 150K parameters against 1.217 for characters at 162K (2 seeds, 30M characters, same validation text and the same per-character metric). Next: try 256 and 1,024 tokens, and repeat at the 97K size. ~30 min per vocabulary size
  2. Train much longer on the 160K model. 100M characters took the 97K model from 1.275 to 1.208. The subword model is the one to scale, since the character model already shows the gain from 30M to 100M characters (1.275 to 1.208 at 97K). Whether it reaches coherent text is unmeasured. estimated ~1 h per 300M-character run if the GPU is otherwise idle
  3. Measure coherence, not just loss. Score samples for grammar, consistency and plot with a model-based grader, the way the TinyStories paper did, so the effect of each change is visible. Loss alone cannot show the gap above. ~30 min to build, needs an LLM API key
  4. Test the long-range memory directly. Check whether the fuzzy rule state remembers a character name 200 characters later with a copy task. Fix that before scaling if it fails. ~1 h
  5. Drop distillation and EMA. Both are measured null results.
