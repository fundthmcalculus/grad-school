# PLAN_REPEAT: reducing repetition in the all-fuzzy model's stories (registered 2026-10-22, before any run)

Owner's request: "I am noticing a lot of repeated words in the fuzzy model outputs. Thoughts on some post-training for that?" then "let's play with 1 and 2": option 1 is decode-time changes (no training), option 2 is sequence-level unlikelihood post-training. The longer-term aim (a larger model on a larger dataset) is in `PLAN_SCALEUP.md`; this plan is about the 99K model.

## Evidence so far
- Repetition on the rated texts of set 24 (`report/coherence24_*`, temperature 0.5, top-k 10, 600 characters, 32 texts per model): repeated word trigrams all-fuzzy old shape 9.9%, F7 8.1%, hybrid H3 7.2%; a content word repeated within six words 6.4% / 6.3% / 5.7%; distinct bigrams 0.785 / 0.813 / 0.824. Correlation of the repeated-trigram share with the rated total over the 96 texts: -0.10 (the earlier 144-text analysis: -0.27).
- Earlier attempts (`PLAN_NEXT.md` item 4, `RESULTS.md`, 30M, old shape, one checkpoint per arm, one rater): token-level unlikelihood in pretraining cut generated repeats 20% and scored 1.6 points *lower* in coherence; repeat-weighted cross-entropy changed nothing; a repetition penalty at decoding was the worst decoder tested (`coherence3`). Reading then: repetition is a symptom of a model with little to say, not the bottleneck.
- The sampler used for every rated set (temperature 0.5, top-k 10) is itself a strong driver of loops; it was chosen for comparability, not for low repetition.

## Metrics (all computed on word-level text, `flm/repeat_metrics.py`)
Repeated trigram share (a word trigram already seen earlier in the same text), a content word (more than three letters) repeated within the last six words, distinct-1 and distinct-2; **plausibility**: the mean negative log-likelihood per token of the generated text under an *independent, better* model, the hybrid H3 at warm-up 1000 (`v72-hybshape300`, 0.9441 bits per character; the same BPE6144 tokenizer), so a setting that buys fewer repeats by sampling implausible text shows up as a higher value; for the post-trained models also validation bits per character (1M characters) against the base and against a cross-entropy-only control. **Rated coherence** (two blind raters, rubric G/C/P 1-5, total 3-15) on the selected arms only; a rated difference under about 0.5 is within the noise (standard error 0.25-0.3 at 32 texts).

## Part 1: decode-time (no training; `flm/decode_sweep.py`)
Model: F7 at 300M (seeds 0 and 1, 0.9660 and 0.9686). 4 story prompts x 12 samples per checkpoint = 96 texts per setting, 600 characters. Settings: **S0** temperature 0.5, top-k 10 (the baseline of every rated set); **S1** T 0.7, k 10; **S2** T 0.8, top-p 0.9; **S3** T 0.7, min-p 0.05; **S4** S0 + no repeated trigram (a token that would complete a trigram already in the text is blocked); **S5** T 0.7, k 10 + no repeated trigram; **S6** S0 + frequency penalty 0.5 over the last 64 tokens; **S7** S0 + repetition penalty 1.3 over the last 32 tokens; **S8** S0 + DRY (a penalty 0.8 x 1.75^(match length - 2) on the token that would extend a sequence already repeated, allowed length 2); **S9** T 0.7, k 10 + DRY.
Candidate rule (fixed now, before the results): a setting is a candidate if its repeated-trigram share is at most 0.6 times S0's **and** its mean H3 negative log-likelihood is at most S0's + 0.25 nats per token. Up to three candidates with the lowest repeated-trigram share go to the blind set, together with S0.
Predictions: no-repeat-trigram (S4, S5) cuts the repeated-trigram share by at least 90% (95%, by construction); at least one setting is a candidate (80%); S1 (T 0.7) cuts the repeated share by at least 25% (65%) at a plausibility cost of at least 0.10 nats per token (60%); S6 and S7 (frequency and repetition penalties) raise the H3 negative log-likelihood by at least 0.30 nats per token without halving the repeated share (50%: the earlier "worst decoder" result); the best decoding setting is rated within 0.5 of S0 on both raters (55%), better by at least 0.5 on both (20%), worse by at least 0.5 on both (25%).

## Part 2: sequence-level unlikelihood post-training (`flm/post_ul.py`)
Start from F7 (seeds 0 and 1). Each step: (1) 8 prefixes of 48 tokens from the training stream (V6144); (2) the model continues each by 96 tokens with its deployed sampler (temperature 0.5, top-k 10; on-policy), without gradient; (3) with gradient, a forward pass over prefix + continuation; every continuation token that completes a word-piece trigram already present earlier in the sequence (prefix included) is flagged; the loss on flagged positions is -log(1 - p(token | context)) (mean over flagged tokens); (4) plus the ordinary next-token cross-entropy on a fresh data batch (16 x 512) so the language model is retained; total = cross-entropy + alpha x unlikelihood. This differs from the 30M experiment: it penalises tokens that continue loops *in the model's own samples*, not any token seen in the last 32 positions of the data context (which also punished legitimate repeats such as names). AdamW, the pretraining parameter groups, lr 3e-3 (5% of the pretraining peak) with 20 warm-up steps and cosine decay, 400 steps (about 10 minutes per run).
Arms (each on both checkpoints): **ctrl** alpha 0 (cross-entropy only, the same steps: separates the effect of extra training from the effect of the term), **ul1** alpha 1, **ul3** alpha 3. Evaluation: validation bits per character; the metrics above at S0 (96 texts per arm) and at the best decoding setting of part 1; the rated set below.
Predictions: ctrl moves validation bits per character by at most 0.002 from the base (80%); ul1 is within +0.003 of ctrl (60%), ul3 within +0.003 (35%); the repeated-trigram share at S0 falls by at least 20% for ul1 (55%) and at least 30% for ul3 (60%); the plausibility (H3 negative log-likelihood) of ul3's samples is worse than ctrl's by at most 0.10 nats per token (55%); combining the best post-trained model with the best decoding setting has a lower repeated share than either alone (80%).

## Rated set (set 25, one blind pass for both parts)
Groups, two checkpoints x 16 texts each (32 per group per rater): **F7 base at S0**, **F7 base at the best decoding setting**, **the best post-trained arm (by the validation-bits guardrail and repeated share) at S0**, **that arm at the best decoding setting**; 128 texts per rater, fresh seeds, two sub-agent raters, scores written before the key is read.
Predictions: the post-trained model at S0 is rated lower than the base by at least 0.5 on both raters (30%: the earlier -1.6 came from token-level unlikelihood in pretraining), higher by at least 0.5 on both (15%); no group differs from the base by more than 1.0 on both raters (70%: the repeated-trigram share is 7-10% of the trigrams; a change in repetition of that size is small against the rubric's resolution); the combination is not rated higher than the better of its parts by 0.5 (75%).
Decision rule: an arm that lowers the repeated-trigram share by at least 30% *and* is rated at least as high as the base (within 0.3) *and* costs at most 0.003 validation bits per character is a keeper; otherwise the finding is again that repetition is a symptom, with the sampler as the cheaper lever.
