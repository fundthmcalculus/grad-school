### ★2. The matrix-free reorder does not work ✅ fixed (documented as a negative result)

`pvat.vat_prim_mst_seq` is the package's only matrix-free VAT reorder. It returns the seed
vertex followed by **every other vertex in ascending index order** — chance-level agreement
(0.001 ± 0.001) with the true ordering, at both float64 and float32.

Cause: `_get_dist(samples, u, vertices[mask])` is typed for scalar indices, so
`np.sum(np.square(diff))` reduces over *all* candidates and returns one scalar;
`key[mask] = <scalar>` gives every candidate the same key and the heap pops in index order.
**Nothing in the package calls it** — it is exported, dead, and wrong.

§3.3.2 described matrix-free operation in the present tense as one of the two ideas lifting
the memory ceiling. It is now separated: in-place is built, matrix-free is not, and the
latter moved to Goal G4. Corroborating evidence found in the repo's own files —
`VAT_TSP_SCALE_FINDINGS.md:61` calls matrix-free the "next wall," and
`performance-novelty.md:61` attributes it to Deshpande & Kumar, i.e. the competitor.

**Nothing in Chapter 3 actually depended on it**: the in-place scheme at float32 reaches
~155,000 points on the host, covering every reported result including the 135,000-point run.

### A5. Table 3.1's headline pair was ~10× stale ✅ fixed — and it resolved into evidence
Prose said mergeVAT 2.56 s at 4,096; the harness said 0.265 s. Not an error: **2.56 s is stage
one (NAFIPS, published) and the harness measures stage two.** The ratio, 9.7×, sits on the
three-arm study's own independently measured `s1/s2` of 7.5–9×. What looked like a
contradiction is §3.3.1's two-stage story corroborating itself, and the chapter now says so.

### A6. The 8,000× projection was wrong twice ✅ fixed
It applied stage-*one* asymptotics to stage-*two* measurements, and discarded the measured
constant (341× predicted vs 48× observed at N = 4,096). Deleted. The three baselines are now
separated explicitly, with the numba-compiled 15–50× named as the defensible one.

### A7. The three-arm table cannot carry the exponent claim ⬜ open — now confirmed twice
Stage two is *slower at N = 1,000 than at N = 2,000*; `s1/s2` runs non-monotone. **A second
independent run reproduced the non-monotonicity** (9.1 → 1.3 → 5.6 → 7.6), so this is a real
property of the measurement, not a one-off. §3.3.1, §3.4 and Appendix A.2.4 all lean on this
table. Needs a larger N grid under the G4 protocol.

### A8. Two different memory ceilings on the same host ✅ fixed — the premise was wrong
Table 3.3 said ≈89,000, Table 3.1 said 135,000. **Neither the 64 GB figure nor my inference
was right**: the host is a 14th-gen i9 with **96 GB**, and 64 GB is a self-imposed working
cap to avoid paging. Corrected throughout (§3.4, Table 3.3, Appendix A.5), and Table 3.3 now
reports both ceilings — 135,000 needs 72.9 GB at float32, inside the machine, outside the cap.

### ★3. Chapter 3 mixes two machines, and nothing recorded it ✅ instrumented, ⬜ unresolved

Adding machine-spec capture to the harness immediately exposed that the `main-d0efefc` suite
ran on the **development laptop** — i7-1185G7, 4 physical cores, 15.5 GiB, Intel Iris Xe,
`powersave` governor — not the 32-core / 96 GB / RTX 4080 workstation the prose describes.
So Table 3.1's swept timing grid is laptop data sitting next to Table 3.3's workstation
memory ceilings and the workstation's 58,000- and 135,000-point reorders.

Consequences: the 45% "cross-run variance" above **may be cross-machine rather than thermal,
and cannot now be decided** because the earlier archives carry no machine record. §3.4 now
labels both machines explicitly rather than implying one. Chapters 4 and 6 are unaffected —
R² is deterministic given code and seeds; only wall-clock is hardware-dependent.

`run_all_tables.sh` now writes a `machine:` block into every `PROVENANCE.txt` (host, OS,
kernel, CPU, cores, RAM, governor, boost, GPU, Python), including on backfills. Resolution of
the underlying mixture is Goal G4: re-take every timing on one pinned host.

## What is genuinely strong, and worth protecting

Unchanged from the first draft of this review, and reinforced by what the session found. Four
findings were retracted by the author's own harness; one upstream bug was found by a study
looking for something else; one silent correctness bug is confessed unprompted in §3.3.2; the
seed-count lesson in G4 is earned from a real 1-in-10 divergence. This session added three
more self-corrections in the same spirit — a second upstream bug, a superseded-code
discovery, and a hardware premise that turned out to be a policy rather than a fact.

Two results improved under `main`, both in the author's favour:

- **Chapter 6's conclusion.** At matched capacity the tuned mixture (0.833 ± 0.024) is
  *nominally ahead* of the flat model (0.824 ± 0.043); best-vs-best the flat leads by 0.028.
  Every gap is inside at least one spread, so the chapter now claims *level* — which is what
  it always wanted to argue — and the tuned mixture is the most stable model in the table.
- **Refinement's decay sharpened.** +0.914 at 0th order, +0.072 at 1st, +0.037 at 2nd: a
  factor of twenty-five. A better *structure before search* datapoint than the archived run.

And one new Chapter 3 result the chapter did not previously claim: **float32 in-place reaches
126,491 points under the cap and 154,919 on the full machine, with an ordering elementwise
identical to float64 across all ten seeds.** A measured doubling of reachable N at zero cost
in exactness.
