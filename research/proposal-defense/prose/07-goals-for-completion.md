# Chapter 7 — Goals for Completion

This chapter states what remains to turn this proposal into a dissertation. I have a real head start — Chapters 3 and 4 are done and published or nearly so, and Chapters 5 and 6 have working code and preliminary results — so the goals here are about finishing, hardening, and, above all, connecting the pieces into one system. The plan is deliberately aggressive: the proposal defense is at the end of 2026 and the final defense is in March 2028, which is fifteen months of research runway, and I intend to use it to produce a substantial and convincing body of results rather than a minimal one.

One thing I have tried to do differently in this version of the chapter, because a committee cannot approve what it cannot check. Every goal below carries a named experiment, a metric, a threshold, and the outcome that would count as refutation. A goal that cannot fail is not a goal, it is a promise, and I would rather be held to the first. Where a goal's honest prediction is a null result, I say so in advance rather than leaving myself room to call it a confirmation afterwards.

## 7.1 The capstone: the integrated pipeline

The integration deliverable is the end-to-end pipeline running as one system. Each chapter proves a stage in isolation — structure discovery in Chapter 3, membership generation in Chapter 5, model synthesis in Chapters 4 and 6, optional refinement in Appendix A — but the claim that the pipeline *is* a pipeline is only demonstrated when the stages are chained: a bare dissimilarity matrix in, a readable fuzzy inference system out, with the speed and scale numbers measured across the whole path. The concrete deliverable is one reproducible driver and one flagship case study carried through every stage from start to finish.

I used to call this the single most important deliverable, and I have stopped, because that ranking does not survive looking at what the document actually needs. The capstone is an *integration* result: it shows the stages compose. Two other items are prior to it in the logical order and are what a hostile reader will reach for first. The baseline adapters of checklist **C1** are one — the speed argument in Chapter 1, Chapter 4 and Chapter 8 currently has no conventional fuzzy method measured beside it, and Chapter 1 §1.1 says so plainly rather than quoting a ratio it has not earned. Goal **G2** is the other, because the coordinate-free regime is the seam this work occupies and it is so far demonstrated on synthetic non-metric matrices built from coordinate data. The capstone is the deliverable that makes the document a *pipeline*; C1 and G2 are the deliverables that make its two headline claims measurable. All three are must-haves; the capstone is not the first among them.

Two specific gaps make the capstone more than a packaging exercise, and both surfaced while writing the chapters up. The first is that Chapter 5's membership functions have never actually been fed to Chapter 6's inference machinery: every result in Chapter 5 is a clustering score, which is a proxy for the fuzzy-model quality the chapter exists to produce. The second is smaller but cuts across everything — the Concrete benchmark is currently run three different ways in three places, so the numbers in Chapters 4 and 6 cannot be read against each other. Fixing that is cheap and removes a real vulnerability.

**How the capstone can fail, stated before it runs.** The experiment is one driver on the UCI shuttle set — Chapter 3's reorder, Chapter 5's membership generation, Chapter 6's inference, Chapter 6's Ruspini export — at ten seeds on shared splits, plus the *same driver* on one of Goal G2's dynamic-time-warping sets so that the coordinate-free path is exercised end to end at least once. The metrics are end-to-end classification accuracy of the FIS built from Chapter 5's memberships, against the Chapter 4 Gaussian construction on identical splits and against CART and a random forest, together with the rule and clause counts of the resulting base. The threshold I will hold myself to: the Chapter 5 pipeline counts as demonstrated if it lands within 0.02 accuracy of the Chapter 4 construction on shuttle, with a rule base no more than twice the size, *and* the same driver completes on a DTW matrix where the Chapter 4 construction cannot run at all.

Three ways that fails, and I would report each as a result rather than as a delay. If the Chapter 5 pipeline is more than 0.02 behind Chapter 4's on shuttle, then §5.4's adjusted-Rand proxy was optimistic and the honest headline is that topological memberships are a *worse* antecedent generator than a per-feature Gaussian mixture on data where both can run — which would leave Chapter 5's contribution as the coordinate-free case only, and would need saying in Chapter 5 rather than buried here. If the driver will not run on a DTW matrix at all, the capstone has demonstrated integration only inside the regime Chapter 4 already covers, and §7.3's coordinate caveat becomes fatal to the capstone rather than a limitation of one dataset choice. And there is a specific mechanism by which the first failure would arrive, which my own notes already predict: the minimax memberships are ultrametric step functions rather than calibrated posteriors, and the phase-6 validation in `gated-minimax-selection/notes/MF_PROGRESS_LOG.md` scores them *worse than crisp zero-one labels* against true Gaussian posteriors. A downstream Takagi–Sugeno–Kang solve weights its rows by firing strength, so a step function may give it less to work with than a Gaussian does. If that is what happens, the fix is the pairing that log already recommends — the multi-scale selector for structure, feature-space kernels for the graded memberships — and it is a design change, not a schedule slip.

## 7.2 Proposed studies

I organize the remaining work as the goals below. Two notes on labels before the substance. Goal G4 was a single row in an earlier version of this chapter and is now five separately tracked items, **G4a–G4e**, because they are four different kinds of work with four different statuses and a committee approving one row was approving all of them; the TODO notes in Chapters 3, 4, 5 and 6 that say "see Goal G4" mean the G4 family, and the one they actually point at is **G4a**. And two checklist items that were not goals — **C1** (the fuzzy baseline adapters) and **C7** (the memory-augmented result) — are promoted to goals here, because Chapter 10 was scheduling neither and both are load-bearing for claims this chapter makes.

### G4a — The measurement protocol (protocol; substantially in place)

This is the consolidation point for the board-wide repeatable-performance standard that recurs throughout the document. Every performance and scaling number, for both scalability and stability, is to be reported under one fixed protocol: pinned clocks and thermals, one identified host, ten seeds, reported error bars, and the numeric stack recorded.

**Most of this is done, and Table 7.1 says so rather than carrying it as future work.** The ten-seed floor is enforced in `reproduce/common.py` and recorded in every run's provenance file (checklist B1); the machine block is appended to every emitted table (B2); timings report ratios with the seconds kept in the companion CSV (B3); and Chapter 3's timing grid has been re-taken on one identified host as the run of record `reproduce/outputs/full-14900hx-r2/`, with §3.4 and Table 3.2 re-quoted from it (B5, B5b). What remains of the protocol is narrower than the goal used to imply: clocks and thermals are still not pinned, the submodule-SHA guard is not in place, and the cross-precision ordering check behind Table 3.3's single-precision column has been taken at one small size and needs repeating near the sizes the ceiling is claimed for.

**One item that used to sit in this goal is discharged, with one of its three arms qualified rather than confirmed.** Chapter 3 previously rested its complexity claim on reachable problem size and treated the cubic-to-quadratic drop as asserted rather than shown, because the timing grid was too coarse to fit an exponent from. Table 3.2 now fits one, on a single identified host, and two of the three arms come out where they should: classical at 3.20 against a theoretical 3, and **stage two at 1.97 against 2**, both stable across five independent runs (3.14–3.21 and 1.93–1.97). Those two are measured, and the chapter says so.

Stage one is the arm I should be more careful about, because the chapter currently reads as though it confirms alongside them. Its fitted exponent is 1.86, stable across 1.84–1.87, and that is not merely short of the $\approx 2.1$ that $O(N^2 \log N)$ predicts — it is *below* the pure quadratic reference of 2. The chapter explains that as the log factor not being resolvable over the range swept, and the explanation is plausible and **asserted rather than shown**: nothing in Table 3.2 distinguishes "a quadratic-log arm whose log factor is invisible over a decade and a half" from "an arm doing less work in this range than the bound allows." So the honest statement is that stage one is *bounded* rather than *confirmed* — the measurement establishes that it is not cubic, which is the claim §3.3.1's progression actually needs, and it does not establish the log factor. Saying more costs almost nothing: report a **constrained fit** beside the free one, fixing the model at $t = c \cdot N^2 \log N$ with $c$ the only free parameter and giving its residual against the free-exponent fit's. If the constrained model fits within noise the log factor is present and unresolvable, which is the chapter's story; if it fits worse than a free exponent of 1.86, the story needs changing. That is one line in the generator and no new compute, and it belongs in this goal because it is the same lesson as the plateau — an unshown explanation, restated confidently, is exactly what cost this chapter two claims.

I am widening this goal to cover *accuracy* claims and not only timing ones, because the seed count turned out to matter more than I assumed. Moving the harness from three seeds to ten retracted a crossover in §4.3.2, refuted that section's central hypothesis outright, and exposed a mixture-of-experts split that diverges to predictions of 10,536 MPa on a target bounded near 82 — a failure that three or five seeds never sampled and that no aggregate over them would have revealed. Ten seeds is now the floor for any *estimate* quoted in this document. The general form of the lesson is that a mean without a spread, over a sample too small to contain the failure modes, is not evidence.

That floor has one deliberate exception, and stating it is cleaner than letting the rule be quietly broken. The large-scale runs — the 58,000-point shuttle reorder, the 135,000-point psychiatric set — are **existence proofs, not estimates**. They answer "can this problem be reordered at all, in what memory, in roughly what time," and the answer to that question does not have a sampling distribution the way an $R^2$ does. Capability and accuracy are both established on the small and mid-size sets, where the seed floor applies in full and where the ground truth to score against actually exists; the large runs exist to show that the same code reaches that size. Re-running them ten times would consume hours of compute to put an error bar on a yes. So the standard is: every accuracy number and every comparative timing ratio gets ten seeds and a spread; a single-shot demonstration of reachable scale gets its hardware, its precision, and its memory footprint recorded instead, and is labeled a demonstration rather than a measurement.

**And it has three places where the document currently breaks it, which I would rather itemize than leave the standard sounding absolute.** A board-wide rule with unnamed violations is worse than a narrower rule honestly stated.

The first is **Table 6.4**, the memory-augmented dynamical-systems result. Its double-pendulum row runs at one fixed `random_state=42`, outside `reproduce/`, emitting no CSV — so it is a one-seed point estimate, and it is neither ten seeds nor a demonstration of reachable scale. It is quoted in §6.3.6, in §6.4, and proposed to the committee in §7.3, which is the worst combination of the three. It is also blocked on a real defect rather than only on effort, since the iterated rollout the row's trajectory half needs returns its initial window unchanged. That is checklist **C7** and it is now a scheduled goal below rather than an item Chapter 10 omits.

The second is the **§6.3.5 optimizer study**. It is at ten seeds, so it meets the seed half of the protocol — but its archive (`reproduce/outputs/opt-hotcold-2026-08-02/`) carries a machine block reading a four-logical-core Xeon virtual machine under Linux, not the run of record's thirty-two-core i9. The host is recorded in the archive and *not* stated in the prose, which is precisely the arrangement that produced this chapter's most instructive error: a repeatable, error-barred, ten-seed measurement that was a property of the machine. The study's conclusion is a negative result about population methods and I do not expect the host to move it, but the chapter should say which host it came from, and the identification sweep archived beside it runs at three seeds rather than ten.

The third is **Appendix A.3**, the `tribble-opt` performance section. Every speedup in it — the truncated-normal sampler, the data-shipping change, the just-in-time local search, the Lin–Kernighan and backend comparisons — is quoted with no seeds, no spreads, no host and no generator. These came from an engineering profiling log, not from the harness, and the appendix should label them as such. The precedent is in my own notes and it is exactly the right size: I once sized an optimization from a profiler, published a 19% speedup, and measured 9.8% on a wall clock afterwards — wrong by roughly a factor of two, because cProfile charges per-call overhead and pandas `__getitem__` has a deep internal call chain. Profiles find hotspots; wall clocks size them. Until those ratios go through the harness they are engineering-log measurements, and A.3 should say so rather than sitting inside a document whose standard is ten seeds and a spread.

One further boundary belongs with the standard rather than with the exceptions, because it is inside the rule and easy to misread as a violation. Appendix A.4's Table A.2 is reproducible bit-for-bit within one host and *not* portable off it: one arm moves by up to 0.043 in accuracy between hosts at identical code, seeds and feature rankings, and it is the ill-conditioned arm that moves. That is the protocol working — it is what recording the numeric stack is for — and the appendix's guidance not to quote that column to four decimals across machines is the right response.

**Why the hardware half of this protocol is not a formality**, stated at length because it is the strongest argument I have for the goal. An earlier version of this section reported that **stage two acquired a fixed cost of roughly ten milliseconds at $N \approx 750$** and ran flat to 3,000, so that the compiled kernel — the one §3.3.1 prefers at import time — was level with the portable fallback between roughly 750 and 1,250 and bought nothing across that band. I called it narrower and more actionable than the goal it replaced, and named OpenMP parallel-region setup as the leading candidate.

**Re-taking the grid on the workstation, which is what this goal asks for, retracted all of it.** Across five runs there stage two is monotone in $N$ — 0.4 ms at 750 rising smoothly to 9.6 ms at 3,000 — and beats stage one by 7.4× to 17.5× at every size in the grid, including 17.3× inside the band said to collapse. The plateau, the parity band, and the claim that the compiled kernel buys nothing are properties of the four-core development laptop, not of the kernel; they are withdrawn from Chapter 3. The quadratic exponent is *better* established as a result, since the plateau had been contaminating that fit — 1.97 where the laptop gave 2.12, a figure Chapter 3 had already conceded was "right for the wrong reason".

The item that survives is smaller and sits on the other host: something on that laptop cost ten milliseconds above a size threshold, and the OpenMP explanation is now the weaker candidate, because thread-startup overhead should be at least as visible on thirty-two cores as on four. A four-core `powersave` governor ramping clocks on thread spawn fits better and is testable by pinning that machine's governor. It is worth chasing only because it is a live example of the failure this goal exists to prevent, not because anything in the method depends on it.

That episode is the strongest argument I have for the protocol, and it is worth stating as a general lesson because the shape recurs. The plateau was measured repeatedly, deterministically, with error bars, at ten seeds — everything the seed-count half of this goal asks for — and it was still an artifact, because every one of those runs was on the same machine. **Repeatability establishes that a measurement is not noise; it says nothing about whether it is a property of the code or of the host.** Only a second host distinguishes those, which is why the harness now records the numeric stack — numpy, scipy, scikit-learn and the BLAS build — alongside the CPU and RAM.

*Decision rule.* G4a is discharged when every numbered table in the document either carries a machine block naming one host at ten seeds, or is explicitly labeled a demonstration with its hardware, precision and footprint, or is named as an exception above with the reason. It fails if a table cannot be brought into one of those three categories — in which case that table comes out of the document rather than being quoted under a standard it does not meet.

### G4b — The eVAT and clusiVAT head-to-head (experiment; not started)

Chapter 3 owes a direct comparison against eVAT (Meng and Yuan 2018) and clusiVAT on identical datasets, and it is the first comparison a reviewer will ask for. It is tracked as checklist **C5** and it is not started, in the specific sense that neither implementation is in hand: this is an obtain-or-write item before it is a measurement item, which is why Chapter 10 now gives it a bar of its own instead of folding it into the protocol's quarter.

*Decision rule.* The experiment is the exact VAT ordering on shared datasets across the swept grid, at ten seeds on the run-of-record host, reporting wall clock, peak memory, and ordering agreement against the serial reference. Two thresholds, because the two competitors fail differently. Against clusiVAT, which samples and is therefore approximate, the claim to test is *exactness at comparable cost*: mergeVAT must reach ordering agreement 1.000 where clusiVAT does not, at a wall clock within one order of magnitude. Against eVAT, which is already exact on a GPU, the claim is narrower and I should say so — I am not claiming to be the first exact GPU VAT and §3.2 concedes that. What is testable is memory: mergeVAT's in-place scheme against eVAT's footprint at matched $N$. It is refuted if eVAT matches the in-place ceiling, in which case Chapter 3's memory contribution collapses to a constant factor and §3.3.2's $\sqrt{3}$ argument is the only thing left standing.

### G4c — The datacenter-GPU re-run (experiment; blocked on hardware)

The pairwise-distance kernel loses to the CPU below 1× at low dimension and at double precision on a consumer card, and §3.3.3 predicts that a card with full-rate FP64 flips it. That prediction is untested and labeled as such, in the chapter and in Table 3.4 (checklist **C8**). This is blocked on access rather than on effort, and Chapter 10 therefore does not give it a quarter — a bar for work that cannot start would be fiction. It is listed with its gate instead.

*Decision rule.* One kernel, one grid, two cards: the consumer RTX 4080 already measured and one datacenter card, at ten seeds, at float64 and float32, across the dimensions where the loss is worst. The prediction is confirmed if the float64 low-dimensional row reaches ≥ 1× on the datacenter card; it is refuted if it stays below 1×, in which case the loss is a property of the *algorithm's* arithmetic intensity rather than of the consumer card's throughput ratio, and §3.3.3's caveat has to be rewritten as a limitation rather than as a hardware artifact. If no card is available, §7.4's fallback applies and the CPU-parallel and single-precision results are reported clearly scoped, which is what Chapter 3 does today.

### G4d — The matrix-free reorder (build; not started)

§3.3.2 had described this as built. It is not: the package's only matrix-free implementation does not produce the VAT ordering at all, and Table 3.3 reports that as a negative result. Nothing in Chapter 3 depends on it — the in-place scheme at single precision reaches about 155,000 points on the host, which covers every result reported — but writing and verifying it is what would carry the method past that, so it belongs here rather than in the completed work. G4d does *not* include a dense-Prim baseline: a code review established that the compiled kernel already is one.

*Decision rule.* Compute each $D_{i,j}$ on demand and verify the ordering elementwise against the serial reference at $N \in \{1{,}000, 2{,}000, 5{,}000\}$ across ten seeds; then report the reachable $N$ under the 64 GB working cap. It succeeds if the ordering is exact at every size and the ceiling moves past Table 3.3's 154,919-point single-precision figure. It is not worth having if the on-demand distance cost makes a reorder past that ceiling slower than the problem is worth — so the second threshold is a wall clock: if the matrix-free path at 155,000 points is more than an order of magnitude slower than the in-place path at the same size, the honest conclusion is that the memory wall was the wrong wall to attack next, and this becomes future work rather than a chapter section. It is a cut candidate for exactly that reason.

### G4e — The general merge operator (open question; partially measured)

This one belongs here because the method is named after it. **The merge itself is unfinished.** §3.3.4's divide-and-conquer stitch works and is measured — Table 3.6 has the principled version recovering an adjusted Rand index of 1.00 across every partition tested, where naive concatenation collapses to 0.47 — but it is a two-way stitch over blocks chosen by farthest-point sampling, not a general merge operator. Three things are unknown and all of them matter for a distributed implementation: whether the stitch composes, so that merging four blocks pairwise gives the same ordering as merging them at once; how the bound on reconstruction error grows under repeated or hierarchical application rather than a single pass; and how to choose block boundaries when the data does not partition cleanly, since farthest-point sampling is a heuristic that the ablation shows is necessary but does not show is sufficient. This is the most open in kind of the G4 family — G4b, G4c and G4d are engineering and measurement, this one is a question. It is checklist **C10**.

*Decision rule.* Composition is the one that can be settled cheaply and it goes first: order four blocks, merge them pairwise in both of the two orders and all at once, and compare the three orderings elementwise on the two-moons and circles constructions of Table 3.5 across ten seeds and a grid of partition counts. The operator composes if all three orderings agree exactly; it does not if any pair disagrees. That is a clean pass-or-fail and it decides the rest: if the stitch does not compose, the half-million-point distributed target is withdrawn rather than carried as an aspiration, the error-growth question becomes moot, and Chapter 3 says that the merge is a single-level operator — which is what Table 3.6 actually measures. I would rather retire that target on evidence than keep it on hope. The error-growth bound and the block-boundary question stay open either way and are named as future work in the dissertation rather than promised here.

### C7 — The memory-augmented result, fixed and measured (build + experiment; not started)

§7.3 proposes carrying the double pendulum and its relatives to the committee as a deliberately aerospace-flavoured demonstration. There is currently no scheduled work behind that promise, and the promise cannot be kept as things stand: Table 6.4's row is one seed at a fixed `random_state`, its $R^2$/RMSE pairs are mutually inconsistent about the target's scale, and the iterated rollout the trajectory claim rests on returns its input unchanged. So either the work happens or the promise comes out, and I am scheduling the work, because the defect is diagnosed down to a one-line slice.

*Decision rule.* Two parts. Fix `MimoGaussianPredictorMemory.predict_trajectory` to slice `window_size + memory_size` rows of history and verify that the rollout advances at the four `(window_size, memory_size)` pairs where it is known to fail. Then wire both scripts — the double pendulum and the Atwood machine — through `reproduce/common.py` at ten seeds so the row is an estimate under the protocol rather than a single run, and re-measure both $R^2$ and RMSE so the target's scale is consistent. The one-step claim survives if the memory-augmented arm still leads at ten seeds with the gap larger than the pooled spread; the *iterated* claim survives only if the fixed rollout beats the memoryless model over a horizon, which no measurement in this document currently supports. If the ten-seed re-measure erases the gap, the section's headline error reduction goes with it and Chapter 6 loses what it currently calls its clearest single result — which is a real risk on a one-seed number and the reason this is scheduled first rather than last.

### G1 — Direct one-pass membership generation (build + experiment; partly built)

Collapse Chapter 5's two-stage select-then-fit pipeline into a single pass, in which each block emits its native membership function, the disjunction recombines them, and the surviving envelope is the model. This is the differentiator, and it feeds the capstone.

**Its status is more advanced and less encouraging than "preliminary" suggests, and the difference matters for what I promise.** Phases one to three of `MEMBERSHIP_ROADMAP.md` are built: memberships are generated directly from the persistence structure with no separate fitting stage and no accuracy cost, the argmax of the generated fuzzy partition reproduces the hard labels exactly at every scale, and each scale is a valid Ruspini partition of unity. Phase five, the one-pass refactor itself, has not been attempted and is plumbing. Phase four — the soft, kernel-weighted band membership, which is the research-interesting piece and the thing I said would fix the small-sample over-segmentation — **has been attempted once, on `feat/mf-phase4-bands`, and it did not fix it.** With the single-block-band drop and a containment-aware merge added, the `single_scale` family cleans up and genuine hierarchies are unregressed, but the `log_separated` family at small $n$ moves from an adjusted Rand index of zero to roughly 0.57, against the flat set-cover's 1.00. The diagnosed cause is not sampling density: single-linkage chains through the diffuse cluster, so one cluster produces about eighteen nested significant blocks spanning birth heights 25 to 180, and birth-height banding shreds that and is then fooled by its own containment test.

So I am not going to write "which I expect to fix the over-segmentation" again. The expectation has been tested and it is currently losing.

*Decision rule.* The experiment is the scaling study's three fixed-structure families — `single_scale`, `many_scale` and `log_separated` from `battery_hierarchical.SCALABLE` — at $n$ = 100, 250, 500, 1,000, 2,000 and 5,000, with the one-pass generator run against both the current two-stage selector and the flat set-cover on identical data. The metrics are the recovered granularity vector, per-level adjusted Rand index of the defuzzified partition against each ground-truth level, and partition-of-unity error where Ruspini normalization is on. Three thresholds: `many_scale` must not regress from granularities [8, 4, 2] at ARI 1.00 at any $n$; partition-of-unity error must stay at machine precision; and `log_separated` at $n \le 500$ must reach ARI ≥ 0.95 with a single band of three clusters, which is the flat cover's answer and the number phase four failed to reach.

What counts as *not* fixed, so that there is no room to declare a partial success: anything below 0.95 on `log_separated` at $n \le 500$. If soft bands land where phase four landed, the conclusion is not that the kernel needs more tuning. It is that birth height is only a clean band coordinate when each cluster occupies a narrow birth range, that for single-level widely-varying-spread data the flat set-cover is simply correct, and that the deliverable is a **single-versus-multi-level gate** that detects one antichain and defers to the flat cover — a smaller and more honest contribution than a better band kernel, and one the evidence already points at. G1 would then ship as one-pass generation over phases one to three plus five, with the over-segmentation fix relabelled as a gate rather than as a membership. I would report that, and Chapter 5 §5.5 needs the same correction, because it still carries the expectation this paragraph withdraws.

### G2 — Real non-coordinate benchmarks (experiment; not started, datasets verified)

Everything topological is so far demonstrated on synthetic data with known ground truth. The core niche — that this works where there are no coordinates — has to be shown on genuinely non-metric domains: time series under dynamic time warping, sequences under edit distance, graphs under a kernel dissimilarity. This is the single most important credibility gap to close, and it serves both Chapter 3 and Chapter 5.

**The design difficulty has to be stated honestly, because it changes what success can even mean.** The natural competitors mostly cannot run here. Deshpande and Kumar's kd-tree and bounding-box methods need Euclidean coordinates by construction; clusiVAT samples a coordinate space; eVAT's GPU front end computes distances from points. Warped time series have no fixed vector embedding — that is the entire premise of DTW — so the coordinate requirement is not inconvenient for those methods, it is unsatisfiable. That is exactly the seam §3.2 claims. It also means **"beat the baselines" is not available as a success criterion in this experiment**, because in the regime the experiment exists to demonstrate there are no baselines to beat. A goal whose criterion is unavailable is a goal that cannot fail, so the criterion has to be something else, and it is four things.

*Decision rule.* The data is already identified and verified in this environment: UCR/UEA classification sets under DTW through `aeon` — Crop (24,000 series, 24 classes), ElectricDevices, StarLightCurves, ECG5000 and FordA — with TUDataset graphs under a graph kernel and the Duin–Pękalska collection, which is distributed *as* distance matrices and so matches the claim most literally, as the second family. The four criteria:

1. **Exactness under real non-metricity.** The mergeVAT ordering on each real dissimilarity matrix must be elementwise identical to the serial reference at every size and every seed, which is what Table 3.7's synthetic rows establish and what its last row cannot. This is the criterion that carries Chapter 3's claim, and it is pass-or-fail. Refuted if agreement is anything other than 1.000 anywhere: the engine would then quietly assume a metric somewhere in its internals, and §3.2's regime claim would be false rather than unproven.
2. **The non-metricity is actually harder than the proxy it replaces.** Report the measured triangle-inequality violation rate on the sets used. Two of them have been checked already and they violate on 29.3% and 16.3% of sampled triples, against the 14% of the fractional-Minkowski stand-in Table 3.7 currently reports. The threshold is that the chosen sets must violate at a rate at least as high as that synthetic proxy — otherwise the new experiment is *easier* than the one it replaces and demonstrates nothing beyond it.
3. **Downstream usefulness against the baselines that can run.** On a dissimilarity matrix, NERFCM given $k$, ConiVAT, single-linkage, beta-plateau and bottleneck-bootstrap all run. Against those, the gated set-cover — discovering $k$ — must land within 0.05 adjusted Rand index of NERFCM-given-$k$ on at least three of the five DTW sets, and `select_coverage_cover` and `select_multiscale` must run on those matrices at all, which per §5.4 they never yet have: the relational block of Chapter 5's driver evaluates only NERFCM. Refuted if the set-cover cannot come within 0.05 on any real non-coordinate set. That outcome would not make the coordinate-free property false — it is a property of the construction, provable by reading the code — but it would make it *useless*, and Chapter 5's niche claim would narrow from "the construction works where the competitors cannot run" to "the construction cannot use coordinates," which is a proof obligation and not a result.
4. **Reachable size on real relational data.** Crop at 24,000 objects is about 4.6 GB as a float64 dissimilarity matrix, squarely inside the regime Chapter 3 exists for, and it is also the natural place to exercise on-demand distance computation, since materializing 288 million DTW pairs is what one wants to avoid. That row is a demonstration in G4a's sense, recorded with hardware, precision and footprint rather than an error bar.

### G3 — The hierarchical mixture, finished and compared (build + experiment; one-shot built)

Implement the EM refinement of the mixture of experts, and benchmark the model family against the baselines a reviewer will demand on identical splits.

**The baseline list is narrowed, deliberately, and Chapter 6 §6.4's version of it should be narrowed to match.** What will be built is ANFIS and a genetic-algorithm-tuned FIS (checklist **C1**, and first in the schedule rather than last), CART, Random Forest, flat TSK, and M5 model trees *if* the M5 decision below goes that way. What will **not** be built is a reimplementation of Fumanal-Idocin et al. (2025) or of the deep TSK fuzzy classifier, and I would rather say so than list them and quietly not do it. Each is a complete published architecture; neither has an implementation the harness can reach; and neither is load-bearing for any claim in this document, because the claims that need a baseline are "faster than conventional fuzzy training," which ANFIS and GA-FIS answer, and "competitive with standard regressors," which CART, Random Forest, M5 and flat TSK answer. Both are cited as related work in Chapter 6 §6.2 and stay cited. If the committee wants one of them reimplemented, the closest competitor to Chapter 6's fuzzy tree is Fumanal-Idocin et al., and taking it on displaces the broadened dataset suite (G3b) rather than fitting alongside it — that is the trade and I would rather name its price than absorb it.

**M5 is not an unrun experiment, it is a dependency fault, and it needs a decision date rather than a slot in a suite.** The generator already imports `m5py` optionally and would fill the row unattended, but `m5py` does not load against scikit-learn 1.9.0, and pinning an older scikit-learn to rescue two cells would move every other number in Chapter 6. So the branches are: upgrade or patch `m5py` against a current scikit-learn; write an M5′ implementation, which means implementing a published algorithm and is potentially a month rather than an afternoon; or drop the row and say in Table 6.2 that M5 is unavailable in this environment. Chapter 10 carries **31 March 2027** as the decision date, before G3's suite is built, because deciding it later means discovering mid-suite that one of four baselines is a build.

*Decision rule for the EM, stated in advance because otherwise every outcome is a win.* This is the goal where the document has pre-absorbed all three answers, and I should say so plainly. §6.2 concedes that a single-layer TSK system is already functionally equivalent to a mixture of experts, which narrows what a hierarchical mixture can claim. §6.3.5 and §6.4 both measure additional search buying progressively less as consequent capacity grows — refinement worth 0.914 at zeroth order, 0.072 at first, 0.037 at second, a factor of twenty-five across the range. And §6.3.3 says that if the EM slips, the one-shot mixture stands as a completed contribution. Left as it is, EM ≈ one-shot reads as confirmation, EM > one-shot reads as the deliverable, and EM-never-runs reads as a de-scope, which is three doors and no experiment.

So the prediction goes on the record first. **I expect EM ≈ one-shot at second order on Concrete, and I expect what it buys to be stability rather than level.** The metric is $R^2$ and its ten-seed standard deviation at matched capacity on shared splits, plus the divergence rate — the fraction of seeds whose predictions leave the target's observed range, which is the failure the seed-9 episode exposed. The threshold: EM counts as a positive result if it moves the mean by at least 0.02 $R^2$ over the one-shot fit *or* cuts the ten-seed spread by at least a third, with zero divergent seeds. Anything inside that band is the null result I am predicting, and I will report it as a confirmed prediction rather than as a shortfall — it is a third independent instance of *structure before search*, alongside §6.3.5's population-method finding and §6.4's refinement decay.

The asymmetry is what makes this falsifiable, and it runs the way that is uncomfortable for me. **The outcome that would embarrass the thesis is the positive one.** If joint EM re-estimation beats the greedy one-shot fit by more than that band, then a global search over the gates *did* find something the structure-first construction left on the table, in my own code, on my own benchmark — and §6.3.5's argument that "once structure is recovered, search has little left to find" needs weakening, in print, rather than being quoted alongside a result that contradicts it. That is the finding I would have to report against myself, and naming it now is the only way the null result counts as a prediction.

### G5 — Output partitioning: reopened (decision; three studies run)

I previously recorded this goal as settled, with quantile boundaries the recommended default on the strength of a three-seed sweep showing quantile's advantage growing monotonically with target skew. Re-running the same experiment at ten seeds refutes that. The sign reverses in every row past symmetry, and the reason is visible in the spreads rather than the means: quantile does not become *less accurate* under skew, it becomes *unstable* — deviations of ±0.99, ±4.45, ±21.2 at the high-skew end, where a handful of catastrophic splits drag an otherwise reasonable distribution far negative. Uniform degrades smoothly and predictably to near-zero instead.

What survives is the diagnosis, and it is worth more than the recommendation it replaces. Bucket starvation is real and measured — uniform's smallest bucket falls to one sample by skew 1.8 and to zero past skew 14 — and so is the correction to my own tail prediction: uniform does *not* hold the tails better, because an evenly spaced bucket in a sparse tail is useless if nothing lands in it. The study also surfaced a bug in the consequent solve, now fixed upstream. But the conclusion I drew from all that — that guaranteed occupancy therefore makes quantile the safe default — does not hold. Quantile removes one failure mode and introduces another. This is the clearest instance in the proposal of the seed-count lesson in G4a: a three-seed protocol produced a clean, publishable, wrong answer.

So the honest status is that neither scheme is safe on a heavily skewed target, and the remaining work is no longer corroboration but a decision: either characterize quantile's instability well enough to guard against it, or accept that a heavily skewed target needs a transform rather than a better partition.

**Estimated effort: about three weeks, and I am correcting a padded figure rather than restating it.** An earlier version of this section said one quarter. That was wrong twice over. The skew sweep is one of the cheapest generators in the harness — it runs in under a minute — so re-running it under a guard is minutes of compute, not hours, and none of the remaining work needs new data or a new experiment. What is left is a framing decision, a guard to implement, and one confirming sweep. Three weeks is generous for that, and Chapter 10's bar is sized to match; the sixty days it previously carried matched neither the quarter this section claimed nor the work the harness actually requires.

*Decision rule.* Run the existing skew sweep with a candidate guard on the quantile path — the simplest being a minimum-occupancy floor on the consequent solve, refusing a bucket whose conditioning is worse than a stated threshold and falling back to the neighbouring bucket's consequent. The metric is the ten-seed standard deviation of $R^2$ at skews above 5, which is where the instability begins. The guard succeeds if it brings quantile's spread inside uniform's at every skew in the sweep without costing more than 0.02 in mean $R^2$ at low skew. If it does not, the recommendation is the other branch — a heavily skewed target needs a target transform, and the partition scheme is the wrong place to fix it — and Chapter 4 §4.3.2 says that instead of recommending a scheme. Either answer closes the goal; what is not acceptable is leaving it open.

### G6 — Interpretability, measured (experiment; not started, and now scoped down)

The interpretability claim should be measured, not asserted. An earlier version of this goal said "either an established interpretability metric or a small expert-audience study," and that disjunction was an escape hatch: the countable half is computable from a fitted tree today, so the goal's real content was the optional half, and no metric was named anywhere in the document. I am picking, and I am picking the version that can actually be delivered inside the runway.

**What G6 is.** Rule counts, clause counts and root-to-leaf path lengths at matched accuracy, filling Table 6.3's pending row — those are read off a fitted model and are owed regardless. Plus a *named* metric family rather than an unnamed one: the semantic-constraint criteria the fuzzy-interpretability literature already uses and this document already cites — Valente de Oliveira's semantic constraints on membership functions, and the interpretability criteria of Guillaume and Charnomordic's partition-generation and FisPro work — evaluated on the Ruspini export of §6.3.4. Concretely that means coverage, distinguishability, normality, and partition-of-unity error, computed per model, alongside the counts. Two of those the construction already satisfies by design and one is already measured at machine precision in the phase-2 membership work, which makes the report a check on a claim rather than a new experiment — and that is the point: the claim is currently *described*, and describing it is what Table 6.3 does today.

**What G6 is not, and why I am dropping it rather than carrying it.** There will be no expert-audience study. No protocol exists, no sample size has been computed, there is no institutional-review timeline behind it, and a result of the form "six domain experts preferred the hierarchy" would not be defensible in a dissertation whose interpretability bibliography is one XAI entry deep. Proposing a human-subjects contribution off that base would be the weakest thing in this chapter. It stays as post-defense work, where it would need a real protocol and a real literature review first. One consequence to name: §6.2 lists "whether a rule base mixing one- and two-dimensional antecedents reads coherently to a domain expert" as a question only a person can answer and points at G6 as its natural home. With the study dropped, that question is unanswered in this dissertation, and §6.2's bound has to be stated as holding for the hierarchy as built rather than as pending a study that is not scheduled. Relatedly, §2.6's position on post-hoc explanation is reframed rather than tested — "post-hoc answers a different question," which needs no experiment — rather than scoped into G6 as a SHAP comparison it cannot support.

*Decision rule.* Compute the counts and the four semantic criteria on the flat FIS, the fuzzy tree, the mixture and the Ruspini export, on Concrete and on one G3b dataset, at matched accuracy. The claim this chapter makes is that the hierarchy buys a readable decision path at comparable accuracy, so the threshold is: at accuracies within one pooled standard deviation of each other, the hierarchy's mean root-to-leaf path must mention strictly fewer variables than the flat model's rules do, and the Ruspini export must satisfy coverage and partition-of-unity exactly. It is refuted if the hierarchy's paths are no shorter than the flat model's rules at matched accuracy — in which case the interpretability-for-accuracy trade Chapter 6 argues for is not a trade at all, it is a loss, and §6.5 has to say so.

### G7 — Adaptive multi-scale (open question; stretch, first cut)

Replace Chapter 5's gap heuristic for band discovery with a model-based criterion — a change-point or barcode-stability test — so that overlapping density scales, which the gap heuristic cannot handle, become tractable. I mark this explicitly as a stretch goal and the first thing I will cut if time runs short.

"Tractable" needs a criterion or it is not a goal, so here is one, and the evidence to beat already exists. *Decision rule.* The experiment is the same three scaling families as G1, plus one purpose-built family with deliberately overlapping log-birth ranges. The metric is the recovered scale count and the per-level adjusted Rand index. The threshold: the model-based criterion must recover the correct scale count at every $n$ from 100 to 5,000 — one band for `log_separated`, three for `many_scale`, one for `single_scale`, where the gap heuristic currently reports a spurious extra coarse band — and reach per-level ARI ≥ 0.95, against the gap heuristic's granularities of [1, 1, 1] and ARI 0.00 at $n$ = 100 and 500 — and 0.57 at $n$ = 250, which is the phase-four kernel band's ceiling too.

It is refuted if a change-point or stability criterion cannot beat the flat set-cover's 1.00 on single-level widely-varying-spread data. In that case the phase-four diagnosis is the right one — birth height is not a usable band coordinate when one cluster chains into eighteen nested blocks spanning birth heights 25 to 180 — and the deliverable is again the single-versus-multi-level gate rather than a better band criterion. That is a smaller result and it is still worth reporting, and it is one more reason this is a stretch goal and the designated first cut: its most likely outcome is a negative result that G1 would also produce.

### G8 — Joint memberships where the structure requires them (retargeted post-defense)

Every membership function in this work is one-dimensional, which is what keeps the rule count linear and the clauses readable — and is a hard expressive limit, since a ring is not the intersection of per-axis intervals. The proposal is to extend to joint two-feature memberships *only* for clusters that have no faithful axis-aligned description, using the topological disjunct count of Chapter 5 §5.3.5 as the detector so the decision is a measured property of the data rather than a preference. The reason it is not a plank: it trades away interpretability, which is the property the dissertation is arguing for, so it is only worth having if such clusters turn out to be rare on real data.

**I am retargeting the construction to post-defense work — a journal extension rather than a dissertation goal — and saying so here rather than carrying a quarter that the schedule does not contain.** An earlier version of this chapter gave G8 one quarter and 2028 Q1, and Chapter 10's Gantt and quarter grid omitted it entirely; that quarter already carries the capstone, G6, G7, the write-up and the defense, and adding a goal to it on paper only would be the kind of thing this chapter exists to stop. Two further reasons make the retarget the right call rather than a convenience. The construction spends interpretability, which is the dissertation's own thesis, so it is the one goal whose success would weaken the argument around it — Chapter 6 §6.2 already flags that tension. And the empirical question that decides whether it is worth having has not been answered: the §5.3.5 disjunct counter has never returned a value other than one on any recorded run, on any battery dataset, in either mode, so nothing in the results establishes that it can even discriminate a two-piece class from a one-piece one.

**What stays in scope is the measurement, not the construction**, and it is cheap: run the disjunct counter over G2's real datasets and report how often a class has no faithful axis-aligned description. That folds into G2's sweep at almost no cost and it is the number that decides G8's fate. If such clusters turn out to be common, the honest conclusion is that this construction is the wrong tool for that data, and I would report that as a limit on the dissertation's scope rather than as a reason to build the extension. If they are rare, G8 is worth building afterwards, on a real example, for the journal version. Either way Chapter 6 §6.2's claim should be stated as holding *without* G8, so that the hole is bounded rather than pending — which also closes a tension Chapter 5 §5.5 and Chapter 6 §6.2 currently both defer.

## 7.3 Application showcases

The flagship case study is the **UCI shuttle set** — the same 58,000-point NASA reentry telemetry that Chapter 3 uses to demonstrate scale. It is chosen for continuity as much as for suitability, and I would rather say that plainly than dress it up: one dataset carried from the reordering in Chapter 3 through membership generation, model synthesis and the final rule base gives the reader a single thread to follow, and it is the set this work has the longest history with. It is also public, which the psychiatric set of §3.3.2 is not, so the capstone is reproducible by a third party end to end. The class structure suits the pipeline too — seven classes with roughly 80% of records in one flight condition, which is exactly the imbalance the complement rule of §4.3.5 was built for, so the open-set behaviour gets exercised rather than merely described.

One limitation of that choice belongs here rather than in a footnote. The shuttle set **has coordinates** — seven sensor channels — so a capstone built on it demonstrates the pipeline end to end but does *not* exercise the coordinate-free regime that Chapter 5's premise rests on. The capstone and Goal G2 are therefore answering different questions, and neither substitutes for the other: the capstone shows the stages compose, G2 shows the stages work where the competing methods cannot run. I note this because "we ran the whole pipeline on the shuttle set" could easily be mistaken for having closed G2, and it does not. This is also why §7.1's capstone criterion requires the *same driver* to complete on one of G2's dynamic-time-warping matrices: without that arm the capstone would demonstrate integration only inside the regime Chapter 4 already covers. The choice is also revisitable; if the IoT sets turn out to make a better showcase once the pipeline is assembled, switching costs a re-run rather than a redesign.

Alongside the primary showcase I will carry the memory-augmented dynamical-systems result — the double pendulum and its relatives — as a deliberately aerospace-flavored demonstration for the committee. **That promise is now conditional on C7 above, and I would rather make the condition explicit than let the sentence stand on a number it cannot support.** Today Table 6.4's row is a one-seed point estimate at a fixed `random_state`, taken outside the harness, with $R^2$/RMSE pairs that disagree about the target's scale, and the iterated rollout the trajectory half needs does not run. Chapter 10 schedules the fix and the harness integration in the first quarter, before anything is built on top of them. If C7's re-measure at ten seeds erases the gap between the memoryless and the memory-augmented arm, this showcase comes out of the defense rather than being presented at a scale the evidence does not reach.

The **BETH** host-telemetry set deserves a sentence here too, because Chapter 4 describes it as the testbed for the open-set claim and every open-set number in that chapter is measured on Glass instead. The obstacle is a research decision before it is a coding one: leave-one-class-out needs at least three classes and BETH is binary, so it needs its own one-class evaluation path, and the comparison against a one-class SVM and an isolation forest has to be scored on a footing that path defines. Chapter 10 gives that decision a short slot in 2027 Q2 with an explicit fallback: if the one-class path is not settled by the end of that quarter, the open-set claim stands on Glass as a *mechanism* validation and not a performance claim, and Chapter 4 §4.4 says so in those words rather than describing a BETH experiment it has not run. Glass at 214 samples across six classes is a stress test, not a demonstration, and either resolution is better than the present arrangement, where the chapter's framing and its tables are about different datasets.

## 7.4 Risks and an honest de-scoping plan

I would rather name the risks than have them named for me.

The nearest one is the prior-art overlap in Chapter 5 with Bonis and Oudot; my mitigation is the three axes of daylight I already stated, and if a reviewer collapses them, the fallback is that the integration and the one-pass membership generation still stand as novel. The EM in Chapter 6 is designed but not built, and if the implementation slips, the one-shot mixture and the fuzzy trees are already complete contributions — with the caveat G3 now states, that a null EM result is my prediction rather than my escape route. The GPU story depends on getting time on a datacenter card; without it I will report the CPU-parallel and single-precision results clearly scoped, rather than overclaim, and G4c is listed with its gate rather than with a quarter for that reason.

**And the baseline tables in Chapters 4 and 6 are the first experiments I owe — which means they are now first in the schedule, not last.** An earlier version of this chapter said the same sentence while Chapter 10 buried those adapters inside Goal G3 in the final quarters, with no bar of their own. That was the largest single inconsistency between these two chapters: the speed and accuracy claims are only as strong as the methods they are measured against, the title and Chapters 1, 4 and 8 all turn on that comparison, and eleven cells across Tables 4.5 and 6.2 — eight in the first, three in the second — read `N/A` until two adapters exist. Chapter 10 now opens with them. Either they are first or this chapter stops calling them first, and I would rather move the schedule than soften the sentence. One thing that helps: Chapter 1 §1.1 already states the speed claim as an absolute — seconds measured, no ratio quoted — precisely because no conventional baseline exists yet, and §7.1's "orders of magnitude" phrasing has been brought into line with it. The comparison is owed; the claim in the meantime is scoped to what is measured.

Two further exposures are worth naming because a committee will find them. My selection gate in Chapter 5 loses outright to more aggressive selectors on the bridge case. I had recorded that as a deliberate conservatism trade, and Table 5.3's coverage column has since weakened even that defence: bottleneck-bootstrap repairs the bridge while still declining three-quarters of the noise, so on this evidence it is better than my gate on both axes rather than trading one for the other. The head-to-head on identical data is the item that settles it. And the non-metric claim that Chapter 3's niche rests on is so far demonstrated only on synthetic non-metric matrices built from coordinate data; until G2 runs on genuinely coordinate-free domains, that argument is structural rather than empirical.

**The de-scoping order, in priority, because the runway is oversubscribed rather than merely tight.** Chapter 10 §10.6 carries the same list and the two must not drift apart. First cut: **G7**, as designated, and its most likely outcome duplicates one G1 would reach anyway. Second: **G3b**, the broadened dataset suite, narrowed from six datasets to Concrete, PhiUSIIL and one added regression set, which is enough to say "more than two problems." Third: **G6's** metric half beyond the counts — the counts themselves are owed and cheap, the semantic criteria are a nice-to-have. Fourth: the **VAT complexity note** of §9.3, which is already conditional on two blocking full-text reads and which §3.3.1 concedes is a modest contribution to a problem someone else has already solved by a better route. Fifth: **G4d**, the matrix-free reorder, since nothing in Chapter 3 depends on it and it buys a regime past 155,000 points that no result in this document occupies. Sixth: **G4e** narrows to the composition test alone, with the error-growth bound and the block-boundary question named as future work.

What I will not cut, and would rather extend the timeline than lose: the **C1** adapters, **G2**, **G4a**, **C7**, and the capstone. Those five are what the two headline claims and the pipeline argument rest on.

The floor under all of this is that Chapters 3 and 4 — the accelerated exact VAT engine and the fast Mixture-of-Gaussians synthesis — are done and defensible on their own. The proposed work extends and connects them; it is not load-bearing for the degree in the way a single make-or-break experiment would be. That is by design.
