# Bibliography

The references for this proposal are maintained as a single BibTeX file, [`../references.bib`](../references.bib), and the final document's reference list is generated from it. This page is a reading guide to that file, grouped the way the chapters use it; it is not a second copy of the data.

A note on provenance and verification. The fuzzy-inference, fuzzy-tree, and mixture-of-experts entries were assembled and adversarially verified during the hierarchical-FIS novelty review. The VAT/clustering, persistence, optimization, and interpretability entries were added while drafting this proposal and then verified against Crossref, arXiv, and DBLP in a dedicated pass (2026-07-31). Notable resolutions from that pass:

- The two former placeholders are real papers: **eVAT** is Meng & Yuan, *"Parallel Edge-Based Visual Assessment of Cluster Tendency on GPU"* (Int. J. Data Science and Analytics, 2018); **Fast-VAT** is Avinash & Lachheb, arXiv:2507.15904 (2025).
- **Bonis–Oudot** (the nearest precedent for Chapter 5) was confirmed as Pattern Recognition Letters vol. 102, pp. 37–43, 2018.
- **AuToMATo**'s title and authors were corrected (Huber, Kališnik, Schnider; *"An Out-Of-The-Box…"*, TMLR 2025).
- **ConiVAT** remains an arXiv preprint only (arXiv:2008.09570) — no journal DOI exists, so none is asserted.

**The true state of the file, counted rather than asserted.** An earlier version of this page said every entry carried a `[V]` or `[S]` marker, that there were no unresolved entries, and that one cosmetic item remained. All three were wrong, and most of the exceptions are entries this proposal leans on. The file holds **70 entries: 45 `[V]`, 23 `[S]`, and 2 still `[?]`.** (The count recorded in `ACTION_ITEMS.md` — "47 `[V]` + 24 `[S]` across 70 entries, zero unresolved" — is wrong on every figure and sums to 71; this page is the corrected count. The `.bib` file's own header legend says the `[?]` entries "were resolved … and promoted to `[V]`", which is also not true of the two below and needs the same correction.) Two distinctions matter more than the arithmetic:

- **`[V]` means metadata-verified, not content-verified.** It certifies that the author list, title, venue, year and DOI resolve against a publisher index. It says nothing about whether the paper has been read. The page previously conflated the two, and one entry makes the difference concrete — see `deshpande2024scalable` below.
- **A `[V]` marker can coexist with an unresolved field inside the entry.** Two do.

**The five entries that are not clean, named individually because four of them are load-bearing.**

- **`arnett2018proposal` — `[?]`, and its title is a placeholder.** The `title` field reads literally `Dissertation Proposal`; the marker asks to confirm the exact title, date and type. This is the same-department parallel-work citation discussed at the end of this page, cited once in §2.1, so it needs a real title before submission.
- **`mullner2011modern` — `[?]`, volume and pages unverified.** The entry is an arXiv preprint record (`arXiv:1109.2378`) with `journal = {arXiv preprint}` and no volume or pages. This is the more serious of the two: it is one of the *two* papers this page itself says §3.3.1's space-bound correction can be assembled from, and Chapter 9 §9.3 says to cite it prominently and early. An unresolved venue on a load-bearing citation is not cosmetic.
- **`parveen2013pvat` — marked `[V]`, but the author field contains a literal placeholder.** It reads `{Parveen, [given name] and Sreevalsan-Nair, Jaya}`; the entry's own note asks to verify the first author's given name and exact title. This is the name-collision citation that §3.3.1 turns on — the published `pVAT` that forced this work's rename to mergeVAT — so it will be read closely, and it currently cannot be typeset.
- **`deshpande2024scalable` — marked `[V]`, and its own note says `FULL TEXT NOT YET READ` / `BLOCKING: obtain full text`.** The metadata is confirmed (*Information Sciences* 664:120324, DOI 10.1016/j.ins.2024.120324). The content is not, and this page previously asserted a claim about what the paper achieves. That is exactly the `[V]`-means-metadata confusion above, and the stakes are recorded elsewhere in the document: `CHECKLIST` **E8** and `ACTION_ITEMS.md` §A both list this read as blocking, because if the paper already states the $O(N)$-workspace result for VAT itself rather than only for MST-iVAT, the Chapter 9 §9.3 note has no contribution left. Until the read is done, this entry supports a citation and not a characterization.
- **`kumar2016incvat` — marked `[V]`, note asks to verify the title.** The smallest of the five and the only one that is genuinely a proof-stage item.

**One further open item is substantive, not cosmetic.** `CHECKLIST` **E7** and `ACTION_ITEMS.md` §E both record a **Zhang-2023 attribution error** — Chapter 6 §6.4 alludes to it as "a small attribution fix in the references", which undersells it: the HFIS reference material misattributes `zhang2023tsk` to "H. Wang et al." when the first author is Zhang. The `.bib` entry itself carries the correct author list and an inline comment saying so, so the fix is in the prose and README that cite it, not in the file — but an attribution error in a survey citation is the kind of thing a committee notices, and it should not be counted alongside an accent-encoding check. So: **two open items, one substantive (E7) and one cosmetic** (confirm the "Kališnik" accent survives the final BibTeX/LaTeX encoding), plus the five entry-level gaps above.

The formerly broken citation is resolved as a *citation*. Chapters 2 and 3 once referred to the kd-tree memory methods only as "[*Information Sciences* 2024]" — a journal name and a year, with no author, title, or DOI. That entry is now `deshpande2024scalable`, metadata-verified, and cited by name in §2.2 and §3.2. It matters more than most, because Chapter 3's argument about which regime is unoccupied depends on what that line of work actually achieves — which is precisely why the full-text read is tracked as blocking and why this page no longer states the paper's result as settled fact.

## On the reference proposals

Two dissertation proposals from this department sit in `research/proposal-defense/` (Pickering 2024, Arnett 2018). They informed the *document structure and format* of this proposal. Neither is a source for any method or framing here; this work was developed independently, and in particular the interpretability position in §2.6 is my own and predates any awareness of Pickering's treatment.

One qualification, so the record is exact. Arnett (2018) *is* cited once, in §2.1, because the FIS constraint set I adopt — triangular membership functions, Ruspini partition, product t-norm, weighted-average defuzzification — appears there in the same combination. That citation marks a parallel in the same department, not a debt: the constraints themselves trace to Ruspini (1969) and de Oliveira (1999), which is what §2.1 cites for them. I would rather note the overlap than have a committee member who has read both documents notice it unremarked.
